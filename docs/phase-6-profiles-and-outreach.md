### Platform-specific notes

| Platform | Priority | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn Company Page** | Do first | Free, you control it, and it is already in your Organization schema `sameAs`. Use the medium description. Add the services list as LinkedIn "Specialties". |
| **Bing Places** | Do first | Free. Bing feeds ChatGPT search, which matters for your AI-visibility goal more than its search share suggests. Mirrors your Google Business Profile. |
| **Crunchbase** | Do first | Free basic profile, strong entity signal for the brand-collision problem. Use legal name Sky Lift LLC with Sky Lift Group as the brand. |
| **Clutch** | High | Requires verified client reviews to rank. Do not create the profile until you have at least one client willing to do a Clutch interview — an empty profile ranks nowhere and looks worse than none. |
| **UpCity** | High | Has a free tier. Asks for service breakdown percentages — estimate honestly. |
| **DesignRush** | Medium | Paid tiers push hard. The free listing is worth having; do not buy placement yet. |
| **GoodFirms** | Medium | Free listing, asks for client reviews and project details. |
| **Sortlist** | Medium | Europe-weighted. Lower priority given a US-only service area. |
| **Agency Spotter** | Medium | Portfolio-heavy. Worth doing once you have case studies with visuals. |
| **G2** | Low for now | Built for software products, not service agencies. Skip unless you productize something. |

**Sequence that makes sense:** LinkedIn, Bing Places and Crunchbase this week —
all free, all fast, all strengthen the entity. Then UpCity and GoodFirms. Hold
Clutch and Agency Spotter until you have a client review and a case study,
because both are review-gated and an empty profile is worse than absent.

### What to avoid

The search for contributor opportunities surfaces a lot of pages titled "Write
for us — construction" and "SEO write for us". Most are link farms: sites that
publish anything for a fee, with no real audience. Links from them range from
worthless to actively harmful, and they are easy to spot — they accept any
topic, they mention pricing early, and their existing posts read as machine-
generated. Skip all of them. Five real placements beat fifty of these.

## 3. Three linkable assets

Ranked by how likely each is to earn links against the effort required.

### Asset 1 — Missed Call Revenue Calculator
**Build effort:** low. **Link potential:** high.

An interactive calculator: enter your average job value, your rough booking
rate, and how many calls you miss in a typical week. It returns the monthly and
annual revenue attached to unanswered calls, and shows the arithmetic rather
than hiding it.

Why this one first: it answers a question contractors genuinely ask, the maths
is simple and defensible, and it produces a personal number people screenshot
and share. Calculators earn links because other people's articles cite them as
a resource.

The honest design decision that makes it credible: it must not invent a booking
rate. Ask the user for theirs, default to a conservative figure, and label the
default clearly as an assumption they should replace.

Lives at `/tools/missed-call-calculator`. I can build it — it fits the existing
static-rendering setup, and the inputs stay client-side.

### Asset 2 — Home Service Texting Compliance Checklist
**Build effort:** low to medium. **Link potential:** medium to high.

A practical checklist covering A2P 10DLC registration, consent capture,
opt-out handling and quiet hours — written for contractors rather than lawyers,
with a plain statement that it is not legal advice.

Why it earns links: it is genuinely hard to find this explained in contractor
language, the rules are confusing enough that people bookmark and share a clear
version, and it positions you on a topic most competitors avoid because it is
unglamorous.

Pair it with the existing A2P and consent posts, which already rank for this
cluster's long tail.

### Asset 3 — Home Service Lead Response Benchmark Report
**Build effort:** high. **Link potential:** highest, but only if the data is real.

An annual report on how quickly home service businesses actually respond to
inbound enquiries — built by submitting identical enquiries to a sample of
contractors across several trades and markets, then measuring time to first
response, channel, and whether anyone followed up at all.

This is the asset that earns the most links, because original data is the one
thing content marketing cannot fake. Trade publications cite original research.

**The honest caveat:** this only works if you actually run the study. A
"benchmark report" assembled from other people's statistics is worth nothing and
would breach the no-invented-data rule this whole engagement has run on. The
methodology needs to be published alongside the findings so anyone can check it.

Realistic scope for a first edition: 100–150 businesses across three trades and
three metro areas. That is a few weeks of work. Do assets 1 and 2 first and keep
this for when there is bandwidth to do it properly.

recorded in October appears in November. A trade publication column can take
longer. The compounding is real but it is slow, and anyone promising otherwise
is selling something.
