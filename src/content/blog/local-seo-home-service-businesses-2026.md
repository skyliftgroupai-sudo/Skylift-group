Search a home service term in almost any city and the same pattern shows up: a map pack of three businesses, then a handful of organic results, then the rest of the internet. If your business isn't in that first group, you're competing for scraps — and most of what determines who's in that first group has nothing to do with generic SEO advice like "post more content" or "use keywords."

For local service businesses specifically, a small number of factors do most of the work. Everything else is secondary.

## Google Business Profile Is the Foundation, Not a Side Task

For any search with local intent — and nearly every home service search qualifies — your [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-home-service-businesses) carries more weight than almost anything on your actual website. Category selection, service area accuracy, review volume and recency, and how completely the profile is filled out all directly affect whether you show up in the map pack. A beautifully built website behind a thin, outdated GBP listing still loses to a mediocre website with a strong, active profile.

## Service-Area Pages Beat One Generic Services Page

A single "Our Services" page trying to rank for every town in a 30-mile radius rarely ranks well for any of them. Dedicated pages built around each specific city or region served — with real, specific content about that area rather than the same paragraph with the city name swapped in — perform meaningfully better, because they match what someone is actually searching for: "[service] in [their specific town]," not "[service] near me" in the abstract.

## Reviews Are a Ranking Factor, Not Just Social Proof

Review count, review recency, and how you respond to reviews all factor into local rankings, separate from their effect on convincing a homeowner to call. A business with 12 reviews from two years ago is at a structural disadvantage against a competitor generating a few new reviews every week — regardless of which business actually does better work. Putting a [review-generation system](/services/reviews) in place is one of the highest-leverage things a local business can do.

## NAP Consistency Still Matters

Name, address, and phone number need to match exactly across your website, Google Business Profile, and any directory listings (Yelp, Angi, Facebook, industry-specific directories). Inconsistencies — a suite number here, a dropped middle initial there, an old phone number still listed somewhere — create confusion that can quietly suppress rankings even when nothing else is wrong.

## Technical Basics That Still Trip Up Contractor Sites

A handful of technical issues show up disproportionately often on home service websites: no schema markup identifying the business as a LocalBusiness or specific service type, slow mobile load times, and missing or duplicate title tags across service and location pages. None of these are exotic fixes, but they're commonly overlooked because they're invisible unless someone specifically checks for them.

## What This Looks Like as a Priority Order

For a business starting from a weak local presence, the order that tends to produce results fastest is: complete and actively maintain the Google Business Profile, build out proper [service-area pages](/services/local-maps), put a system in place to generate reviews consistently, fix the technical basics, then layer in backlinks and broader content once the foundation is solid. Skipping ahead to backlinks or blog content before the foundation is in place tends to produce disappointing results, because the ranking factors that matter most for local search haven't been addressed yet.
