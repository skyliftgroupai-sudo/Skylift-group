Yes. If your home service business sends texts to customers from a standard 10-digit phone number, US carriers require you to register through A2P 10DLC. This is not optional in any practical sense — unregistered business traffic gets filtered or blocked outright, and that includes the appointment confirmations and missed-call replies you depend on most. Registration covers three things: your business identity, the campaign you are running, and the use case behind it. Carriers process it on their own timeline, usually several business days.

## What does A2P 10DLC actually mean?

A2P stands for application-to-person: messages sent by software rather than typed by a human on a phone. 10DLC means 10-digit long code, which is the ordinary phone number format your business already uses.

Put together, A2P 10DLC is the framework US carriers built to distinguish legitimate business texting from spam sent through normal-looking numbers. Before it existed, businesses sent automated texts through regular numbers and carriers had no way to tell a plumber's appointment reminder from a scam. The answer was registration: tell us who you are and what you are sending, and we will deliver it.

The important consequence for a contractor is that this is a carrier requirement, not a software feature. Switching texting platforms does not avoid it. Any platform that lets you send business texts without registration is setting you up to be filtered.

## What happens if I text customers without registering?

Three things, in escalating order of damage.

**Messages get filtered.** Not bounced with an error — silently dropped. You see the message as sent, the customer never receives it, and nobody tells you. This is the failure mode that costs the most, because you keep operating as though your reminders are arriving.

**Your throughput gets throttled.** Unregistered traffic gets the lowest priority and the tightest rate limits. Send to twenty customers and some arrive hours later, which defeats the entire purpose of a [missed call text back](/services/missed-call-text-back) that is supposed to land within a minute — and [missed calls are expensive](/blog/what-missed-calls-cost-contractors).

**Your number gets blocked.** At the far end, carriers block the number entirely. Recovering from this is slow, and in the meantime the number on your trucks does not deliver texts.

The pattern worth understanding: the damage falls on your transactional messages first. Confirmations, reminders, en-route notices — the operational messages your scheduling depends on. Businesses usually discover the problem when a customer says they never got the reminder, which is long after it started.

## What does registration actually involve?

Three records, in order.

### Brand registration

This identifies your business: legal name, EIN, business address, website, contact person. The EIN has to match what the IRS has on file — a mismatch is the most common reason a brand registration gets rejected, and it is usually something small like an abbreviated company name.

### Campaign registration

This describes what you are sending. Message types, expected volume, how customers opt in, and how they opt out. You submit sample messages, and they need to look like what you will actually send.

### Use case approval

Carriers assign your campaign a use case — customer care, account notifications, marketing, mixed. Different use cases get different throughput and different scrutiny. A contractor sending appointment reminders and occasional seasonal offers is usually a mixed or customer care use case.

## How long does A2P 10DLC take?

Brand registration is often same-day to a couple of days. Campaign approval typically takes several business days, though it can run longer if a carrier queries something.

The practical consequence: start registration first, before building anything else. We begin it at the start of every SMS project precisely because it is the one step whose timeline we do not control. Everything else — writing sequences, connecting the calendar, setting up the shared inbox — can be built while it processes.

The common mistake is treating registration as a final step. A business builds the whole system, goes to launch, and then waits a week for approval it could have started days earlier.

## What gets a registration rejected?

The rejections we see most often are mundane.

**EIN and legal name mismatch.** The name on the registration has to match IRS records exactly. "Smith Plumbing" when the IRS has "Smith Plumbing and Heating LLC" gets rejected.

**Sample messages that do not match the use case.** Submitting a plain appointment reminder while describing a marketing campaign, or the reverse.

**No visible opt-in mechanism.** Carriers check that customers can actually consent. If your website form does not mention texting anywhere, a reviewer has no evidence consent exists.

**Missing opt-out language.** Marketing messages need clear opt-out instructions. Sample messages without them get queried.

**A website that does not match the business.** If the registered business is a roofing company and the site is a parked domain, that gets flagged.

None of these are hard to get right. They are just easy to get wrong when someone fills the form in quickly.

## Does A2P 10DLC replace getting consent?

No, and this is the distinction that matters most.

Registration is a carrier requirement about message delivery. Consent is a legal requirement about whether you are allowed to send to a particular person. They are separate, and satisfying one does nothing for the other.

You can be fully registered and still be in breach for texting someone who never opted in. You can have perfect consent records and still have messages blocked for not registering. A compliant setup needs both.

In practice that means: register through A2P 10DLC, capture express written consent at the point you collect phone numbers, keep records of that consent, honor opt-outs promptly, and keep marketing messages separate from transactional ones. None of this is legal advice — confirm your obligations with your own counsel — but it is the baseline any competent provider should be handling.

## Do I need to register if I only send appointment reminders?

Yes. The requirement is about how the message is sent, not what it says. Automated messages from a 10-digit number need registration whether they are marketing or purely operational.

If anything, operational messages are the stronger reason to register. A filtered marketing blast costs you an offer nobody saw. A filtered appointment reminder costs you a no-show, a wasted truck roll and an annoyed customer.

## I have been texting customers already. What now?

Plenty of contractors have been sending texts from a business line for years without knowing registration existed. If that is you, nothing catastrophic has happened — but the fix is worth doing before it starts costing you.

**Check whether your messages are actually arriving.** Ask a handful of recent customers whether they got their appointment reminder. If several say no, you are already being filtered and did not know.

**Register before you grow the volume.** Filtering scales with volume. A business sending ten messages a day may slip through for a long time; the same business sending eighty after a marketing push will not.

**Do not respond to filtering by switching numbers.** Rotating to a fresh number to escape filtering is a spam pattern, and carriers treat it as one. It makes the problem worse and puts your new number at risk too.

**Sort out consent at the same time.** If you have been texting without a documented opt-in, registration is the natural moment to add consent capture to your booking process and start keeping records.

The honest summary: registration is administrative work that produces no visible improvement when it goes well. The return is that your messages keep arriving as you grow, which is invisible right up until it is not.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is A2P 10DLC required by law?

No. It is a carrier requirement, not a legal one. The distinction matters because the consequence is not a fine — it is that your messages stop being delivered. Separately, there are real legal requirements around consent and opt-outs, and those do carry penalties. You need to satisfy both.

### How much does A2P 10DLC registration cost?

Carriers charge a one-time brand registration fee plus a recurring campaign fee, and the amounts vary by carrier and use case. Your messaging platform usually passes these through. The costs are modest relative to a single missed job, which is the right way to think about the comparison.

### Can I avoid registration by using a toll-free number?

Toll-free numbers use a different verification process rather than no process at all. They have their own requirements and their own review. For a home service business the bigger issue is that a toll-free number is not the number on your trucks, which fragments the customer conversation.

### What if I only text a few customers a day?

Volume does not exempt you. Carriers filter on registration status rather than message count. A small operation sending twenty reminders a day is subject to the same rules as one sending thousands.

### Does switching texting platforms mean re-registering?

Usually yes. Registration is tied to the platform and the number. Moving platforms generally means registering the campaign again, which is worth knowing before switching — plan for the gap.

### Who handles registration, me or my provider?

A provider should handle it. We submit the brand, campaign and use case, deal with queries, and start the process at the beginning of the project rather than at launch. You supply the business details and the EIN, which is the part only you have.
