Most home service business owners who've worked with a marketing agency before have at least one bad story — a long contract, vague reporting, and a monthly invoice that never quite explained what it paid for. That experience makes owners understandably cautious about signing with anyone new, which is fair. A few specific red flags tend to predict a bad outcome well before the contract is signed.

## 1. No Clear List of What's Actually Included

"Full-service digital marketing" or "comprehensive marketing package" without a specific, written list of deliverables is the single biggest warning sign. A serious agency can tell you exactly what's running for your monthly fee — how many blog posts, what the ad management includes, what reporting looks like — without vague, all-encompassing language standing in for specifics.

## 2. Locked Into a Long Contract Before Seeing Results

A 12-month contract signed before any work has proven itself puts all the risk on you. Agencies confident in their results can typically offer shorter initial commitments or clear exit terms — long lock-in periods are more often a sign the agency needs the contract to guarantee revenue than a sign the work will actually perform.

## 3. You Don't Own Your Own Data

If the agency disappeared tomorrow, could you export your customer list, review history, call tracking data, and website? If the honest answer is no — because everything lives inside a platform only they control — that's a serious problem. Your leads and customer relationships should belong to your business, not be held hostage inside an agency's proprietary system.

## 4. Reporting Is Vanity Metrics, Not Booked Jobs

Impressions, reach, and "engagement" are easy numbers to report and easy to make look impressive. What actually matters is how many calls, leads, and booked jobs came from the marketing spend. An agency that leads every report with vanity metrics and buries or omits booked-job numbers is either not tracking what matters or doesn't want you to see it clearly.

## 5. No Experience in Your Specific Trade

Marketing a restaurant, an e-commerce store, and an HVAC company all require genuinely different approaches — different search behavior, different urgency, different trust factors. An agency that's generalist across every industry often applies the same generic playbook everywhere, missing details that matter specifically to trades: emergency response, licensing and insurance display, service radius, and seasonal demand swings.

## 6. Pushy About Contracts, Vague About the Work

If a sales call spends more time on urgency ("this pricing is only good today") than on specifics about your business and what they'd actually do for it, that's worth noticing. A genuine fit involves real questions about your service area, current lead sources, and goals — not a scripted close.

## 7. No Willingness to Show Real Examples

Ask to see an actual [chatbot flow](/services/ai-chatbots), a real ad account structure, or a sample report before signing anything. An agency confident in its work will show it. Hesitance to share specifics, or only offering polished case study slides instead of anything concrete, is a reasonable reason to keep looking.

## What to Ask Before You Sign

A short, direct list works better than a long interview: what exactly is included this month, what's the contract length and exit terms, who owns the data, what does a monthly report actually show, and can I see a real example of the work before committing. Agencies that answer these plainly and specifically are a meaningfully different category from ones that answer in generalities.
