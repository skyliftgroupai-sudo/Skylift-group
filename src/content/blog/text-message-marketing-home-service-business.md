Text messages get opened. That's the entire case for SMS marketing in one sentence — open rates for text messages are dramatically higher than email, and they're read within minutes rather than sitting in an inbox indefinitely. For home service businesses, where timing often matters as much as the message itself, that alone makes it worth a closer look.

## Where Texting Genuinely Outperforms Other Channels

A handful of specific use cases consistently work well for home service businesses: appointment confirmations and reminders that reduce no-shows, [missed-call text-back](/services/missed-call-text-back) that catches leads who would otherwise call a competitor, seasonal maintenance reminders (furnace tune-ups before winter, AC checks before summer), and quick [review requests](/services/reviews) right after a completed job. All of these share a common thread — they're short, timely, and expected, not a surprise promotional blast.

## Where It Backfires

Texting works against you when it's treated like an email list — frequent promotional messages, generic discounts, or content unrelated to the service relationship. Customers opt into texting for practical updates about their own service, not a marketing channel, and treating it otherwise produces opt-outs and complaints faster than almost any other channel, precisely because texting feels personal in a way email doesn't.

## The Compliance Piece Most Businesses Skip

SMS marketing in the U.S. is governed by TCPA rules, which require explicit opt-in consent before sending marketing texts, a clear way to opt out of every message, and proper handling of consent records. This isn't optional paperwork — non-compliant texting creates real legal exposure. Any text marketing system should have consent capture and opt-out handling built in from the start, not added as an afterthought.

## A Practical Starting Point

For most home service businesses, the strongest starting use cases are the operational ones — appointment reminders, missed-call text-back, and review requests — because they're expected, useful, and don't require building an opted-in marketing list from scratch. Promotional texting (seasonal offers, service reminders framed as a discount) can be layered in afterward, once there's a properly consented list to send to, and kept infrequent enough that it doesn't erode the trust the operational texts built.
